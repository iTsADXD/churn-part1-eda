# Business Memo

**To:** Product, Marketing, and Customer Support Teams  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** What should be investigated before launching a retention campaign  
**Date:** 2025-10-01

---

## Overview

Before launching a retention campaign, the company should investigate the behaviour patterns that appear to be most strongly associated with churn in the dataset. The analysis suggests that not all churned customers are alike, and a blanket campaign is likely to waste budget.

---

## 1. Recency should be checked before spending on retention

Customers who have not purchased for a long time appear to be much more likely to churn than recently active customers. This suggests that recency is one of the strongest early warning signals in the dataset.

**What to investigate:**
- How quickly churn risk increases after 30, 60, and 90 days of inactivity
- Whether all inactive customers should be targeted equally, or only the recently slipping ones

---

## 2. Web/app disengagement looks like an early warning sign

Customers with very low session activity in the last 30 days appear to churn much more often than highly active customers. This means churn may begin before the customer completely stops buying.

**What to investigate:**
- Whether declining sessions can be used as a trigger for re-engagement
- Whether customers stop browsing first and then stop purchasing

---

## 3. Loyalty enrolment may reduce churn risk

Customers in higher loyalty tiers appear to churn less than customers who are not enrolled. At the same time, a large share of the customer base has no loyalty tier at all.

**What to investigate:**
- Whether increasing loyalty enrolment among repeat customers reduces churn
- Whether unenrolled repeat buyers should be targeted with loyalty sign-up nudges

---

## 4. Paid acquisition channels may be bringing weaker customers

Customers acquired through some paid channels appear to churn more than customers from organic or referral sources. This could mean the business is acquiring lower-intent users through certain campaigns.

**What to investigate:**
- Whether paid channel targeting is too broad
- Whether onboarding and first-purchase experience differ by channel
- Whether customer quality should be measured alongside acquisition volume

---

## 5. Discounts alone may not solve churn

The data suggests that customers who churned did not receive meaningfully lower discounts than retained customers. This means discounting may not be the main lever for improving retention.

**What to investigate:**
- Whether discounts are attracting one-time, price-sensitive buyers
- Whether product experience, support quality, or recommendation accuracy matter more than pricing

---

## 6. Returns and support friction may be hidden churn drivers

High return behaviour and support-ticket issues may indicate product-fit problems, quality concerns, or poor post-purchase experience. These signals should be treated as potential churn warnings instead of only operational issues.

**What to investigate:**
- Whether customers with high return rates churn more often
- Whether negative support experience or slow resolution is followed by inactivity
- Which product categories generate both returns and churn

---

## Recommendation

The company should avoid launching a one-size-fits-all retention campaign. Instead, it should first validate the major churn-risk patterns in:

- recency,
- web/app engagement,
- loyalty enrolment,
- acquisition channel quality,
- returns/support friction.

A segmented retention approach is likely to be more effective than discounting every at-risk customer in the same way.
